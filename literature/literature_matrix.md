| Fischer Black, Michael C. Jensen & Myron Scholes (1972). **The Capital Asset Pricing Model: Some Empirical Tests.** In Michael C. Jensen (ed.), *Studies in the Theory of Capital Markets*. Praeger. | Monthly NYSE stock and market returns; portfolio tests for January 1931–December 1965, with earlier observations used to form portfolios. | Beta-ranked portfolios; time-series regressions of portfolio excess returns on market excess returns with an intercept; cross-sectional CAPM tests. | High-beta portfolios have negative intercepts and low-beta portfolios positive intercepts, contrary to the traditional CAPM. | Source for excess-return definitions and market beta as covariance with market excess returns divided by market excess-return variance. This project uses weekly rather than monthly returns; it does not replicate their portfolio-sorting tests. | Section I: excess-return and beta definitions; Section II.D: data and monthly portfolio construction; Section II.E.1 and Table 2: time-series results. | Not specified; [paper](https://www.efalken.com/LowVolClassics/blackjensenscholes.pdf). | [The Capital Asset Pricing Model - Some Empirical Tests (1972).pdf](<pdf/The Capital Asset Pricing Model - Some Empirical Tests (1972).pdf>) |
| Lieven Baele & Juan M. Londono (2013). **Understanding Industry Betas.** *Journal of Empirical Finance*, 22, 30–51. | Daily industry excess returns for 30 U.S. industry portfolios; 1970–2009. This project uses the 12-industry classification and weekly returns. | DCC–MIDAS and kernel beta estimation; structural-break analysis. | Industry betas change over time. A common structural break is identified around March 1998. | Supports analyzing time-varying market betas and comparing historical periods over a long sample. This project uses weekly rather than daily returns to balance observations within short crisis periods and short-term fluctuations. Does not supply this project's crisis dates. | p. 30, abstract: time-varying betas and March 1998 break. Exact data-section and time-horizon page references require full-text verification. | [10.1016/j.jempfin.2013.02.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jempfin.2013.02.003) | [Understanding Industry Betas (2013).pdf](<pdf/Understanding Industry Betas (2013).pdf>) |
| John Luke Gallup (2019). **Added-variable plots with confidence intervals.** *The Stata Journal*, 19(3), 598–614. | Methodological article illustrated using Stata’s automobile dataset (auto.dta); the main regression example uses 74 observations. No financial-return sample. | Partial regression (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell theorem); separate OLS regressions remove the other covariates from both variables. Added-variable plots display the residual relationship; avciplot adds confidence intervals around the fitted line. | Correlation between the two residual series equals partial correlation. The residual-regression slope equals the corresponding coefficient in the full regression and is distinct from the correlation coefficient. Plots can reveal influential observations. | Supports estimating utilities–technology partial correlation as the Pearson correlation of residuals from separate regressions of weekly sector excess returns on the same market excess returns, each with an intercept and the same observations. Confidence intervals and added-variable plots are not part of the current project specification. | pp. 601–602, Section 2.1: partial regression and residual correlation; p. 606, Section 4: separate residual regressions and degrees of freedom; p. 607: confidence-interval formula and 74-observation example. | [10.1177/1536867X19874236](https://doi.org/10.1177/1536867X19874236) | [Added-variable plots with confidence intervals (2019).pdf](<pdf/Added-variable plots with confidence intervals (2019).pdf>) |
